Where Do Good Opportunities Come From?

Good opportunities rarely come from trying to think one up. Here's where they usually start, and how to tell whether one is any good.

Coming to YouTube soon

About this episode

Before Gymshark, Ben Francis tried apps and a website. What worked was a problem he had himself: he couldn't find gym clothes that fit, so he started sewing his own in his family's garage.

This episode shows where good opportunities usually start: a real problem people already pay for, wait for or work around, in a place you know better than most, at a moment when something has changed. It looks at research on user entrepreneurs, then introduces MERIT, the five questions this series uses to judge an idea, and runs the Gymshark garage through it.

The five MERIT questions

For each one, write down what would have to be true, how good it looks, and how you know.

  1. M
    MarketWho is the customer, how many are there, and will they pay?
  2. E
    EdgeWhy you, and what stops others copying you once it works?
  3. R
    ReturnsDoes the price cover the cost, and is it worth it for you?
  4. I
    ImplementationCan you build it, reach the people who want it and deliver?
  5. T
    TeamDo you have the skills it takes, and who do you need to bring in?
